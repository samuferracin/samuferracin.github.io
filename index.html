Samuele Ferracin

I am a PhD candidate in Mechanical Engineering and Applied Mechanics in the Architected Materials Laboratory at the University of Pennsylvania. My research interests center on reprogrammable metamaterials and the design of their static and dynamic mechanical properties. Before Penn, I earned B.Sc. and M.Sc. degrees in Aerospace Engineering from Politecnico di Torino (Italy) and worked on superconducting bolometer sensors and LIDAR hardware.

Research

In my current research, I develop mechanical systems that can morph, remember, and adapt to external stimuli through nonlinear mechanical behavior. I combine reduced-order modeling, FEA (Abaqus and COMSOL) of instabilities, structure-preserving machine learning, MATLAB/Python, wind-tunnel experiments, and extensive 3D printing (consumer FDM/SLA, microfabrication, and Direct Ink Writing).

In Progress
Bistable Plate Dynamics in Flow
Samuele Ferracin, Yinjie Wang, Dengge Jin, Saheli Patel, Jordan Raney
In draft, 2026
PDF

In this project, we study the fluid-structure interaction between a near-wall von Karman vortex street and a bistable plate with one side immersed in flow, using a potential-flow model and wind-tunnel experiments. Understanding the dynamic behavior of the plate when excited by vortices at different shedding frequencies, including nonlinear oscillation regimes such as snap-through, subharmonic resonances, and chaotic response helps us design metamaterials that can exchange energy with vortex structures, either enhancing or suppressing them.

Structure-Preserving Machine Learning for Bistable Plate Dynamics
Samuele Ferracin, Helen Nguyen, Nat Trask, Jordan Raney
In progress, 2026

In this project, we extend the accuracy and predictive power of the potential-flow model already developed with a structure-preserving learning framework. The nonlinear dynamic behavior of a bistable plate immersed in a KVS flow becomes extremely complex and cannot be fully modeled without computationally expensive CFD simulations. The goal is to retain the physics and stability structure of the potential-flow model while improving prediction by learning non-dissipative and dissipative structures from experimental data.

DIW 3D Printing of Conductive Core-Shell Liquid Crystal Elastomer Fibers
Samuele Ferracin, Aravind Bhola, Jacob Jameson, Jordan Raney
In progress, 2026

In this project, we are developing Direct Ink Writing 3D-printed core-shell fibers with a liquid crystal elastomer shell and a conductive EGaIn, silver-flake, and elastomer ink core. The aim is to create soft artificial muscle fibers for robots and prosthetics that better mimic the compliance, actuation, and integration of human muscle.

Micro-3D Printed Bistable Units for Controlled Cell Deformation
Samuele Ferracin, Luc Capaldi, Alexander Bolano-Campos, Mistica Lozano Perez, Ottman Tertuliano, Jordan Raney
In progress, 2026

This project uses Nanoscribe 3D printing to fabricate micro-bistable units that impose controlled, constant deformation on biological cells. The platform is designed to mechanically program cell deformation and then enable imaging of the resulting cellular response and reconfiguration under load.

Phonon Controlled Mechanical Memory via Pinning and Depinning of Transition Waves
Samuele Ferracin, Dengge Jin, Vincent Tournat, Jordan R. Raney
Under review at PRB, 2026
PDF / arXiv / BibTeX

In this project, we developed a new mechanical memory concept where local defects pin kink transition waves in multistable metamaterials. Boundary phonon excitations are then tuned to depin the kink, offering a scalable route to information storage and mechanical computing without local point actuation.

Marty Bathgate, Phillip J. Ansell, Samuele Ferracin, Dengge Jin, Saheli Patel, Jordan Raney
AIAA SciTech Forum, 2026
PDF / Publisher / BibTeX

This project investigates passive gust load alleviation by coupling aerodynamic loading to snap-through of a bistable hinge on an airfoil flap.
